# Sources and data provenance

**The budget in this game is the City of Hollister's actual FY 2026-27 Approved
Budget.** Fund totals, every General Fund revenue line and every department total
are taken from the published document and reconcile to it exactly. What remains
estimated is the game layer: what each policy option would save or raise, how
service levels respond, and the council.

**General Fund expense by department.** The budget document reports by fund and
expense category, with the department carried in the middle segment of each
account code (`101-‹dept›-‹object›`). Those cost centres are summed into the
city's own named departments, as listed in the
[FY 2024-25 Budget Book](https://hollister.ca.gov/Administrative%20Services/FY%202024-2025%20COH%20Budget%20Book_FINAL.pdf)
(Administrative Services, City Clerk, City Manager, Community Development,
Engineering, Fire, Information Systems, Parks & Recreation, Police, Public Works).
The groupings sum exactly to the published $56,588,184:

|---|---|---|

| Parks & Recreation | $1,988,613 | 8050, 5005, 5010 |
| **Total** | **$56,588,184** | |

Cost centres 2205 and 2500 are identified as Fire and Police respectively from
their own line items: 2205 carries $1.3M of overtime against $7.09M of regular
salary (constant-staffing shift coverage, and about $142K per funded position
across Fire's 50 positions), while 2500 carries $0.5M of overtime, the $1.11M
911 dispatch contract and a records-management software contract, at about $100K
per position across Police's 67. The split also matches the FY 2024-25 Budget
Book, where Fire's contractual costs ($1,243,101) run well above Police's
($757,250).

One caveat on department naming: the budget document does not print department
names beside the account codes, so the names above come from matching cost-centre
line items to the FY 2024-25 Budget Book's department sections. The **amounts**
are official and sum exactly; the **names attached to them** are a reading of the
city's account structure.

## What is still estimated

These are **game-design values**, not city figures:

- **What each policy option saves or raises**, except three that come straight
  from the adopted budget: the $11M interfund loan, the $1.2M contingency and the
  $500K events line. Four more are derived from official bases and labelled as
  such — the hotel tax increase (2 points on $975,000), the half-cent sales tax
  (half of Measure W's $9,682,000), the business licence modernisation (against
  $240,000 collected today) and cost-recovery fees (against $3.7M of building
  permits).
- **All five service meters** and every movement in them. These exist so the
  trade-offs are legible. They are not forecasts.
- **Growth rates** in the three-year outlook: revenue +2.2%/yr, expenditures
  +4.2%/yr. Directionally right for a California city facing CalPERS rate
  increases and salary steps, but not a city projection.
- **The five council members**, who are deliberately fictional archetypes — "The
  Fiscal Hawk", "The Labor Ally" and so on. They are **not** portrayals of any
  real Hollister council member, and no real person's name, record or position is
  used.
- **Department funding floors and ceilings**, which are playable bounds rather
  than any city policy.

## Keeping this honest in-game

The briefing links to a full "Where these numbers come from" breakdown, the Budget
panel carries a source note, and the scorecard repeats the official/estimated
split. Policy cards drawn from real council actions or real budget lines are
tagged **"Actually happened"** with a note saying what the document shows.

Every entry in `data/hollister-fy2027.json` carries a `confidence` field:
`official`, `derived` or `modeled`.
