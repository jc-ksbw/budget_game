# Sources and data provenance

**The live game cites City of Hollister sources only.** Every source link on every
*card* points at `hollister.ca.gov`. The two staffing sources on the Police and Fire
sliders are the City's own Council packet portal and a recording of a Council
meeting — see the citation-host note below. BenitoLink and other reporting were used
as research — to find documents, confirm dates and catch errors — and are listed at
the bottom of this file, but they are not cited in the game.

Four kinds of fact live here, kept apart:

1. **Official budget figures** — from the City's published budget documents.
2. **Documented options** — things the City has adopted or discussed, each with a
   status, a City source and a date printed on the card.
3. **Benchmark options** — revenue tools other California cities use that Hollister
   has **not** proposed, sized from Hollister's own published lines and labelled
   "Common elsewhere" so they can never be mistaken for City proposals.
4. **Game-design values** — the five service meters and the five-year growth rates.

## General Fund composition

Every revenue line and department total comes from the FY 2026-27 Approved Budget
and reconciles to it exactly. The budget reports by fund and expense category,
carrying the department in the middle segment of each account code
(`101-‹dept›-‹object›`); those cost centres are grouped into the City's named
departments as listed in the
[FY 2024-25 Budget Book](https://hollister.ca.gov/Administrative%20Services/FY%202024-2025%20COH%20Budget%20Book_FINAL.pdf).
Cost centres 2205 and 2500 are identified as Fire and Police from their own line
items (2205 carries $1.3M of overtime on $7.09M of salary, ~$142K per funded
position across Fire's 50; 2500 carries $0.5M of overtime, the $1.11M dispatch
contract and records-management software, ~$100K across Police's 67). **The amounts
are official; the names attached to them are a reading of the account structure.**

### What each amount kind means

- **City figure** — the City published this exact number.
- **Derived** — arithmetic on a published City line. The card states the base and
  the percentage, and says the depth is this game's illustration rather than a City
  decision.
- **Benchmark** — a tool other California cities use, sized from a published
  Hollister line. Each basis ends with *"not a Hollister proposal or estimate."*

## The specific corrections

**The loan** — a **financing event, not a budget option**. It is no longer a card the
player can pick: it lives in its own `financing` block in `data/actions.json`, renders
as a read-only event card at the top of the Revenue tab, and **never reaches
`totals()` at all**. Borrowed cash is not revenue and does not shrink an operating
deficit, so the game does not let it.

What the Council actually adopted, from the **October 2, 2026** agenda and the adopted
minutes for **item 5.2** (carried 3–0, moved by Picha, seconded by de Anda):

> "a short-term interfund loan of $18M from the City's Impact Fee Funds (Fund 304 –
> AB 1600 Traffic Impact Fee and Fund 205-Park Dedication Impact Fee) to the General
> Fund ($11M) and Water Fund ($7M), payable in full by June 30, 2027, at an interest
> rate of 3%-4%."

| | |
|---|---|
| Total borrowed | **$18,000,000** |
| General Fund | **$11,000,000** |
| Water Fund | **$7,000,000** |
| Interest | **3%–4%** a year |
| Due in full by | **June 30, 2027** |
| Status | Approved October 2, 2026, carried 3–0 |

> **The rate is a range, not 4%.** The adopted motion says **"3%-4%"**. City staff have
> separately been quoted at a flat **4%**. The card shows the range, because that is
> what the Council voted on, and records the 4% quote beside it.

> **No interest total, no schedule, no split.** The agenda carried three attachments —
> the staff report, the **interfund loan agreement**, and an **FY27 one-year short-term
> loan amortization schedule**. The City's portal returns **403** for all three. No
> total interest figure, no installment schedule, and **no allocation of interest
> between the General Fund and the Water Fund** is shown, because the adopted action
> does not provide one.

> **Two citations, by request.** The card cites the **adopted minutes** (which carry the
> motion, every figure on the card, and the 3–0 vote) and the **loan agreement**, with a
> note saying the portal does not serve it so nothing is drawn from it. The agenda item,
> the staff report, the amortization schedule and the October 12 infographic were all
> dropped as redundant with the minutes or unreadable.

The card carries **no infrastructure penalty** — no City document shows a capital
project delayed because of it — and no meter effect of any kind, since it is outside
the game's scoring entirely.

> **Rate discrepancy, preserved.** City staff have been quoted putting the rate at
> **4%**; the adopted resolution has been described as stating **3–4%**. The
> resolution is not published on the City website and the agenda portal is
> JavaScript-rendered, so both are recorded rather than one being chosen.

**Measure V** — passed Nov 5, 2024, effective April 1, 2025. The City's ballot
question: *"a four percentage point (4%) increase to transient occupancy tax,
increasing the total tax levied on hotel occupants from 8 to 12 percent … generating
approximately $300,000.00 annually."* It is **8% → 12%**, not 10% → 12%. Because it
is already enacted and already inside the $975,000 of hotel tax in the adopted
budget, **it is not offered as a card** — there is no decision left to make. The same
applies to the July 2026 building permit and user fee update, already inside the
$4,211,000 of licences and permits.

**The 1% sales tax** — the October 2, 2026 action **only authorised staff to pursue
special state legislation**. It imposed no tax, set no rate and placed nothing on a
ballot; legislation, a later Council action and voter approval would all be
required, and the City Manager estimated roughly six months just for the legislation
and a fiscal analysis.

The card carries **$9,682,000/yr**, inferred by matching the existing 1% Measure W
tax — **not an official forecast** — with the rate moving **9.25% → 10.25%**. The
game counts a full year of collections against the adopted-year gap, which is enough
to close it on its own. None of the steps above could complete inside FY 2026-27, so
the card is tagged **Needs legislation, council and voters** and its text says the
collections could not begin until all of them cleared.

> **Why the State has to act first.** Hollister's **9.25%** is the statewide base plus
> a full **2.00%** of local transactions-and-use taxes — the **City's own 1%**
> (operative 04-01-2008) and **San Benito County's 1%** safety tax (operative
> 04-01-2019), both listed in CDTFA's district-tax publication. **Revenue and Taxation
> Code section 7251.1** reads: *"The combined rate of all taxes imposed in accordance
> with this part in any county may not exceed 2 percent."* Hollister is therefore at
> the statutory ceiling and **cannot put another transactions-and-use tax on the
> ballot at all**. The Legislature would first have to pass a special statute lifting
> the cap for Hollister; only then could the Council call an election. State approval
> is **not an alternative** to voter approval — it comes before it. The card carries
> this as a rate note. The statute is named in the text rather than added as a
> citation, because the game's `sources[]` are City documents only.

**The Oct 14 layoffs** — noticed, then **rescinded**. *Updated Oct. 27, 2026.*

On **October 21, 2026** the Council took up a resolution *ratifying the rescission of
Resolution No. 2026-111* — the fiscal emergency the layoffs rested on — together with
*direction regarding the employees placed on administrative leave*. Then, on
**October 26**, it voted **unanimously in closed session to rescind the layoffs of 35
city workers**, reversing the decision of the now-former city manager.

The card carries status **Rescinded**. It is **not selectable**, carries **no dollar
figure**, imposes **no meter penalties**, and is skipped inside `totals()` even if
something contrives to select it — a check forces it on and confirms neither gap, no
meter and no sub-total moves. The money line reads *"No savings counted — rescinded."*

> **What the card does not claim.** Not a permanent rehire: the jobs are described as
> **retained for now**, because the vote was taken in closed session and **no City
> record of it has been posted**. No revised payroll figure is shown, because none has
> been published. The deficit figures are **unchanged**. And the card notes that the
> City will hold **weekly budget meetings every Monday**, and that **if major savings
> cannot be found, layoffs may be reconsidered**.

> **On the count.** **35** is the figure in the October 27 KSBW report. The City's own
> October 14 letter to SEIU Local 521 named **16** positions; the card says so and says
> the two are not the same measure, since the letter covered SEIU-represented staff
> only. The earlier derived figure of **$3,617,997** — 16 names averaged and applied to
> 25 positions — is **gone from the card entirely**, and a check asserts no trace of
> that arithmetic remains.

> **The one news citation.** The reversal happened in closed session and the City has
> posted nothing, so the **KSBW report of October 27** is the only source that exists
> for it. It is cited with a note saying exactly that. This is the single exception to
> the City-documents-only rule, and the verification script allows it **by exact URL and
> only with that note**, so no other outlet can slip in.

**The voluntary separations** (July 2026) were not reversed, so that card stays
**locked on**. It has no published savings figure, so nothing lands on the board
before the player chooses anything: the game opens at the full adopted **$6,363,284**.

The figure below is the one the card **used to carry**, kept here as a record of how it
was built. It is no longer shown in the game. It was **derived, not published**. The City's October 14, 2026
*Notification of Staffing Reductions* to SEIU Local 521 — signed by the City Manager
— lists **16 employees** being laid off, plus one frozen vacancy, with a combined
annual payroll of **$2,315,518**, an average of **$144,720** each. That average
applied to **25** positions gives **$3,617,997**.

> **What that number is not.** It is gross payroll, not General Fund savings: five of
> the sixteen are water operators and one is at the airport, paid from enterprise
> funds. Employment severs November 13, 2026, so FY 2026-27 carries only part of a
> year. Severance and leave payouts land against the first year. And **25 is not a
> City count** — the letter covers SEIU-represented staff only, and the City has
> published no citywide total. The card says all of this.

> **Superseded.** The Council rescinded the layoffs on October 26, 2026, so the card
> no longer carries this figure or any other. It is recorded here only so the earlier
> arithmetic is not lost.

> **Where the letter came from.** The City has not posted it on hollister.ca.gov. The
> public copy is an [Instagram
> post](https://www.instagram.com/p/DedlTzsFP_7/?img_index=1) carrying both pages. It
> is cited as what it is — a City of Hollister letter — with a note saying where the
> copy lives. This is the **only** citation in the game that is not hosted on
> hollister.ca.gov, and the verification script allows it by exact URL so that nothing
> else can slip through.

The City separately states the voluntary separations "did not generate sufficient
savings". **No savings amount was published for them**, so that card still shows none.

## Vacancy and staffing context on the sliders

The Police and Fire sliders carry a dated vacancy snapshot. **No other slider does**,
because the source does not break the numbers out that way.

From the City's AB 2561 **"Vacancy Status"** presentation in the **June 15, 2026**
Council packet, reporting **FY 2025-26**:

| Group | Budgeted FTE | Vacancies | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Citywide | 189 | 22 | 11.64% |
| SEIU | 53 | 2 | 3.77% |
| Firefighters union | 44 | 2 | 4.55% |
| Police Officers Association | 52 | 10 | 19.23% |

**What is shown where.** The Police slider shows the POA row and notes that the unit
spans **sworn police, records and support, and animal control** — staff said most of
those vacancies were sworn officers but **did not give an exact count**, so no split
is shown. The Fire slider shows the firefighters-union row. **The citywide and SEIU
totals are not attached to any department**; they live in `staffingSnapshot` for
reference only. The named units account for 149 of the 189 budgeted positions; the
remaining 40 sit in groups the snapshot does not break out.

**Proposed staffing targets**, from the **June 16, 2026** Council budget discussion:
the City Manager proposed seeking **11 additional Fire positions** and continuing to
recruit for **seven Police positions**. These appear **only when that department's
funding is moved above baseline** — they are about adding money, so they stay out of
the way until someone does. Both are labelled **"Proposed target"** with their date,
and carry a caveat that they are *not adopted hires and not a guarantee any post gets
filled*. The same trigger governs the slider's own raise text.

The always-visible block is deliberately compact: unit, date, counts, a filled/vacant
bar, the scope caveat where the source gives one, and a one-line citation.

**What the game does not do with this.** Vacancy counts are never converted into
dollar savings or a cost-per-position figure — a test asserts there is no `$`,
"per position" or "savings" anywhere in the staffing data. Raising Police or Fire
funding is described as *supporting the proposed staffing plan*, and both sliders
say in terms that **funding a position is not the same as filling it**.

**Dating.** Both blocks are tagged *FY 2025-26, as of June 15, 2026* and carry a note
that the City has not published an updated count for FY 2026-27, so this is **not**
current staffing. If a later City report confirms updated numbers, replace the
`staffing` blocks in `data/hollister-fy2027.json` rather than editing these in place.

> **Retrieval caveat.** Neither source could be opened from this build environment:
> the eScribe portal answers with a browser-verification challenge and the meeting
> host with a Vercel security checkpoint. The figures are recorded **as supplied**
> rather than read off the documents. All four vacancy rates are internally
> consistent (22/189, 2/53, 2/44 and 10/52 each match their stated percentage
> exactly), and the citywide 189 sits close to the 191 FTE reported for FY 2025-26
> after 31 vacant posts were cut — likely a different count basis. Worth a read
> against the originals before anyone relies on them.

> **Citation-host note.** These are the only two sources in the game not served from
> `hollister.ca.gov`. `pub-hollister.escribemeetings.com` is the City's own Council
> agenda and packet system, so the AB 2561 presentation is a City document. The
> June 16 link is a **third-party host of a City Council meeting recording** — a
> record of a City proceeding rather than a City-published document, and it is
> labelled that way in the data.

## What the game does not claim

**No reserve is modelled.** There is no opening reserve balance, no cash-flow model and
no loan-repayment model in this game, so it cannot say what the City would have in hand.
A year that ends in the black is labelled a **projected annual surplus** — never a
reserve, and never scored against the City's 20% reserve policy target. Reserve scoring
and the reserve term in the community weights were removed; see the Scoring table in
[README.md](README.md) for where those 5 points went.

**No verified structural deficit.** $6,363,284 is the **adopted FY 2026-27 General Fund
budget gap** — the difference between the adopted budget's expenditure and revenue
totals. The game has not classified the City's own lines as recurring or one-time, and
some of them include contingency and capital spending, so it does not call that figure,
or any revised figure, a verified structural deficit. What the game *can* type honestly
is the measures the player adds: the **ongoing gap (game estimate)** is what is left
once the player's one-time money is taken back out. The five-year outlook is labelled a
**game estimate** built on the stated growth rates, not a City forecast.

**No unknown amount is charted as $0.** A selected revenue measure with no published
figure gets no bar. It is listed under the chart as **"Amount not published"**, with a
line saying it is not charted and does not change the gap. Chart labels, tap readouts
and accessibility text never show it as $0.

## Schema

`data/actions.json` carries per card: `status` (`adopted` / `discussed` /
`exploratory` / `benchmark`), `amountKnown`, `amount` (**null** when unpublished —
never 0), `amountKind` (`published` / `derived` / `benchmark`), `amountBasis` or
`unpublishedNote`, optional `cashBridge`, `conditional`, `locked` (already carried
out: selected from the first screen, cannot be toggled, survives Start over),
`rateNote`, `updatedLabel`, `rescinded` (reversed by the Council: shown for the
record, never selectable, skipped inside `totals()`) and `amountTiming`, and
`sources[]` with title, publisher, url and date.
A source may carry a `note` where a document is linked but was not readable.

`financing[]` is a **separate top-level array** for events that move cash without
changing the budget. Nothing in it is selectable and nothing in it reaches `totals()`.
Each entry carries `label`, `name`, `asOf` + `asOfLabel`, `statusLine`, `principal`,
`fromFunds`, `advances[]` (`fund` + `amount`), `rate`, `rateNote`, `dueDate`,
`purpose`, `repaymentLine`, `note`, `explain` and `sources[]`.
Sources must be on `hollister.ca.gov` or the City's own agenda portal
`pub-hollister.escribemeetings.com`; the verification script allows exactly one
other URL, the SEIU letter, by exact match.
`data/hollister-fy2027.json` carries `deficitLabel`, `deficitSource` and a separate
`revisedDeficit` block.
